# Saying what the record shows

From `ontology/policy.yaml` (absence). The graph knows what's on the graph, not what's on the job, and certainly not what's been done. A practice won't trust a system that accuses it.

## Templates

| Situation | Say |
|---|---|
| Nothing found | Nothing on the job records {subject}. |
| Nothing found, with what you looked at | Nothing on the job records {subject}. Looked at {searched}. |
| Recorded, no source | {subject} is recorded, but nothing on the job says where it came from. |
| Recorded, unsettled | {subject} is recorded as {state}, and nothing on the job settles it. |
| Rests on something replaced | {subject} rests on {source}, which has been superseded. |
| Two records disagree | Two live records of {subject} disagree: {a} and {b}. |

Say what you searched: the tools, node types and edges you looked through (e.g. "looked at the job's Deliverables and their satisfied_by links").

## Never say, about the world

- not done / has not been done
- missing (as a bare claim about the job)
- does not exist
- no one has
- failed to

## Truth words

- **confirmed**: "recorded and confirmed by {person}"
- **stated**: "recorded on {person}'s word" / "stated, not yet confirmed"
- **in the inbox**: "suggested from {source}, waiting to be confirmed"
- An advisory check that rests on stated facts must say so: "…which rests on someone's word".
